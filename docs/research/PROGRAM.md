# The Star Gate lineage

A working history of the U.S. government remote viewing effort, 1972 to 1995. This file exists so product decisions can point at what the program actually did rather than at folklore. Citation keys refer to `SOURCES.md`.

## Two programs in one

From the beginning there were two distinct efforts that shared people and methods but had different customers and different standards of evidence [PUTHOFF-CIA] [AIR-1995].

The research program lived at SRI International from 1972 and moved to Science Applications International Corporation (SAIC) in 1991. Its product was controlled experiments, statistics, and protocol development. Its funders rotated through CIA, DIA, Army, Navy, NASA, and others.

The operational program lived in the Army and later DIA at Fort Meade, Maryland. Its product was intelligence collection against real targets using viewers trained by the research side. Operational sessions answered taskings, not hypotheses.

The platform mirrors this split. Research series carry sequential targets, judging pools, and displacement instrumentation. Operational-style taskings carry encrypted tasking numbers and evaluation against ground truth when feedback arrives.

## Timeline of program names

| Years | Name | Owner | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1972-1975 | (unnamed CIA contracts) | CIA/OSI, CIA/ORD at SRI | Swann magnetometer demo, Perceptual Augmentation Techniques contracts [PAT-REPORT] |
| 1973 | SCANATE | CIA | "Scan by coordinate." Coordinate-tasked viewing, the origin of CRV cueing [PUTHOFF-CIA] |
| 1977 | GONDOLA WISH | Army ACSI | Systems-exploitation survey that located Army personnel with viewing aptitude |
| 1978-1982 | GRILL FLAME | Army INSCOM + SRI | First operational unit at Fort Meade, joined with the SRI research effort |
| 1983-1985 | CENTER LANE | Army INSCOM | Sponsored Swann's CRV training contract, protected by MG Stubblebine |
| 1986-1990 | SUN STREAK | DIA | Fort Meade unit under DIA, the 1986 CRV manual is its training document [CRV-MANUAL] |
| 1991-1995 | STAR GATE | DIA + SAIC | Umbrella name for operations and the SAIC research contract [AIR-1995] |

In 1995 Congress directed CIA to take the program back and evaluate it. CIA commissioned the American Institutes for Research review, accepted its recommendation, and terminated the program [AIR-1995].

## People the platform's history rests on

Researchers and founders.

- Harold E. (Hal) Puthoff. Laser physicist. Founded and directed the SRI program from 1972 to 1985. Author of the founding reports and the retrospective the README cites [PUTHOFF-CIA] [PAT-REPORT].
- Russell Targ. Laser physicist, co-founder at SRI. Co-author of the Perceptual Augmentation Techniques reports and the 1974 Nature paper on information transmission under sensory shielding [PAT-REPORT].
- Edwin C. (Ed) May. Physicist. Joined SRI in 1975, took over the research program in 1985, moved it to SAIC in 1991, ran it until closeout. The SAIC experiments are the core of the evidence Utts assessed [UTTS-1995].

Viewers.

- Ingo Swann. Artist and the program's central subject-theorist. Triggered CIA interest with the 1972 magnetometer demonstration, proposed coordinate tasking (SCANATE), and developed the six-stage CRV method that the 1986 manual codifies [PUTHOFF-CIA] [CRV-MANUAL].
- Pat Price. Former Burbank police commissioner. Produced the West Virginia Site and Semipalatinsk gantry crane results that kept CIA funding alive. Died in 1975 [PUTHOFF-CIA] [PAT-REPORT].
- Hella Hammid. Photographer, recruited as a control subject, became one of the most reliable viewers. Her nine-target outbound series produced odds against chance beyond a million to one [PUTHOFF-CIA].
- Joseph McMoneagle. Army viewer 001, recruited into GRILL FLAME in 1978. Primarily an ERV practitioner. Awarded the Legion of Merit partly for intelligence produced in viewing sessions. Continued as a research subject at SAIC.
- Paul H. Smith. Army officer, trained by Swann, drafted the 1986 CRV manual that this platform implements [CRV-MANUAL].
- Lyn Buchanan. Army viewer and the unit's database analyst, later a CRV trainer. His session database work is the closest historical analog to this platform's metrics layer.
- Ed Dames. Army operations and training officer in SUN STREAK, later took CRV commercial. A cautionary example the manual's monitor rules guard against, since aggressive monitoring produces AOL-driven sessions.

Command and management.

- MG Albert (Bert) Stubblebine. Commander of Army INSCOM, sponsor and protector of CENTER LANE. His retirement in 1984 pushed the unit toward DIA.
- F. Holmes (Skip) Atwater. INSCOM officer who stood up GONDOLA WISH and GRILL FLAME, recruited the first Fort Meade viewers, and served as operations and training officer for a decade.

Evaluators.

- Jessica Utts. Statistician, UC Davis. AIR reviewer who concluded that an anomalous information effect is statistically established across SRI and SAIC data [UTTS-1995].
- Ray Hyman. Psychologist, University of Oregon. AIR reviewer who agreed the results are not explained by chance or by flaws he could identify, but held that a psychic explanation is premature without independent replication [AIR-1995].
- M. D. Mumford, A. M. Rose, D. A. Goslin. AIR authors of the 1995 evaluation that recommended termination, largely on operational-utility grounds [AIR-1995].

## What the record actually supports

Three findings from the primary record shaped this product.

First, protocol integrity was the program's strongest asset. The SRI reports lean on double-blind targeting, complete logging of every experiment before judging, and unedited data packages [PAT-REPORT]. Where later operational practice drifted from that rigor, the record gets weaker. The platform enforces the rigor in software.

Second, judging is the score. The research program's statistics come from independent judges rank-ordering transcripts against target pools, not from anyone's impression of a striking match [UTTS-1995]. LLM assessments in this platform are labeled as advisory second opinions for exactly this reason.

Third, the AIR closeout faulted vague, unfalsifiable operational product and thin process instrumentation [AIR-1995]. Both reviewers, despite disagreeing on interpretation, wanted better process data. Research-scale metrics on every session is the direct answer, and it is why the schema in `METRICS.md` exists.
